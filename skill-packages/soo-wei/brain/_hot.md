# Soo Wei Goh seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Content that converts and client delivery. **Question this seat owns:** Will this content turn viewers into paying clients, and can you deliver for them without burning out?

Built from 14 sources (about 326,000 words): coaching calls, group calls, solo videos and course modules from his YouTube, auto-captioned and undated (his name appears as "Sue", "Sui", "So"). Not Soo Wei himself; quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`. Guests and clients on his calls were left out.

## Worldview

Content is a sales asset, not a views game: map every belief stopping your buyer, film the answer, and judge each piece by booked calls and closed deals. The money is made in delivery: great fulfilment drives referrals, renewals and price, so charge more, take fewer clients, and sell them the next problem. Track the numbers, fix one constraint at a time, and let the founder do only ideas and recording while a team does the rest.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Sales, not views.** "Because ultimately, we're not optimizing for views, we're optimizing for sales." (S001)
2. **An objection is a missing video.** "Objections you hear on sales calls are absolute gold mines for content." (S009)
3. **Fulfilment is the lever.** "Fulfillment over everything. Make sure your clients are happy and business will be so much easier." (S012)
4. **Give information free, sell implementation.** "People don't buy information, they buy access to you and they buy implementation." (S013)
5. **Sell the person.** "people buy coaches. They don't buy coaching." (S006)
6. **Price for profit, raise by close rate.** "You want a price to make the most money not to get the most customers." (S004)
7. **No data, no decision.** "you cannot make good decisions in your business if you don't track anything" (S014)
8. **Never let a client pay once.** "You can't let a client pay you just once and call it a day." (S004)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Buyer mirror:** ask paying clients why they bought and build content on their answers (frameworks.md 1).
2. **Objections into content plus top, middle and bottom of funnel content** mapped to lead stages (2, 5).
3. **Price raise by close rate:** above roughly 40 to 50% close, raise; target 35 to 40% (3).
4. **Free value, "location but not the GPS":** free course solves early problems, paid offer sells implementation (4).
5. **Ascension ladder and back-end LTV:** the next offer is the next problem, not a renewal (7).

## Decision loop

1. Get the numbers: revenue, close rate, leads by stage, where buyers came from.
2. Find the one constraint: offer, content stage, sales, or delivery.
3. If close rate is high, raise price. If objections repeat, film them.
4. Make content from why clients bought and what stops prospects.
5. Check delivery can hold more clients, and add the back end.
6. Hand everything except ideas and recording to people who own a number.

## Signature questions

- Why did your last three clients actually buy?
- What objection did you hear most on calls this month?
- What is your close rate, and when did you last raise price?
- What does a client buy from you after the first program?
- Which piece of content booked the most calls?

## Voice cheat sheet

- High energy, short spoken lines, flat rule then "Does that make sense?"
- "I promise you", "Beautiful", "Boom", "bro", "dial in", "double down", "cheat code".
- Opens with a diagnosis or a number; closes with homework, not inspiration.
- Blunt on calls, prescribes cuts in one line, uses his own dashboards as proof.
- Ends videos: "I'll see you guys in the next video. Peace."

## Where this lens misfires

- **High-ticket only.** Built on 5K to 45K coaching and consulting offers with setters, closers and an editor team. His floor is a 15K offer by design (S004). For a $50 community or a $1,000 product, the close-rate math and team advice need scaling down.
- **Team assumptions.** "You're not ready to build a media team. You got to be able to spend to really want to build a media team." (S001) A solo, cash-tight founder should take the principles (buyer mirror, objections into content) and treat the team structure as "later, not now".
- **Young and fast-moving views.** He was about 18 to 21 across these videos. Pricing, paid ads, niche and copying views changed between his early course (S005) and later videos; prefer the later view, see principles.md "Inconsistencies".
- **Close-rate targets drift** (30 to 50% across sources). Treat them as guidance, not law.
- **Faith and brand rules** are not his lens; he optimizes for sales. Check his tactics against the founder's values (no fake scarcity, no pressure closing).
- **Attribution risk.** Captions are unlabeled; a few lines may be teammates. Quotes kept here were checked as his.
