# Positions: Soo Wei Goh

Built from the six extract batches over S001 to S014 (coaching breakdowns, group calls, course modules and solo videos). 39 topics, most-used first. All sources are undated, so every line says "undated". Where his view changed, both versions sit side by side with a note on which looks newer and why. S005 is an early course (he says he is 18 or 19, offers at 4K to 10K); S004 and S001 look latest (cheapest offer 15K, 400K a month talk, price raised to $45,000). Topics with no evidence say so.

## Content that converts (what content is for)
- **Stance:** Content exists to remove the specific beliefs that stop a prospect buying and to pull ICP leads, not views. General value makes you one more creator.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "Organic content as a whole is actually very simple. You essentially just want to map out every single belief that's holding your client, your prospect back from working with you" (S001)
  - "Because ultimately, we're not optimizing for views, we're optimizing for sales." (S001)
  - "you can't judge this based on views." (S004)
  - "The success of your YouTube is how many leads you generate and how many sales you can actually generate from that YouTube channel" (S006)
  - "Remember, the only reason why you aren't selling more of your offer is because people don't believe that you can do it for them." (S009)
  - "you have to have intentionality behind your content because if you don't, you're just going to be on the algorithm" (S010)
  - "if my if my revenue isn't increasing and my view count is increasing, that means I'm talking to the wrong type of people." (S010)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S003, S004, S006, S007, S008, S009, S010, S013).
- **Read:** consistent across every era. The test he applies to any content plan is how much of it answers a buying objection (inferred from S001, S003, S009, S010).

  - "if you get a specific objection on a sales call nine times out of 10 it's because your content failed to do its job" (S003)
  - "The best ideas that I found are objections from sales conversations." (S006)
  - "Objections you hear on sales calls are absolute gold mines for content." (S009)
  - "the I need to think about it objection is purely a lack of proof. It's purely a lack of proof." (S009)
  - "What are the objections and what is stopping your client from buying from you? Film reels on that." (S010)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S006, S009, S010, S012, S013, S014).

## Client interviews, testimonials and proof
- **Stance:** Client interviews are the best sales asset he has. Film one per ICP, show live reactions not screenshots, and package them to look like value content.
- **Evidence (undated):**

  - "client interviews are by far the easiest to generate sales" (S006)
  - "if you film a 16-year-old client interview, you're going to attract the bunch of 16 year olds" (S009)

- **Gap:** in S012 he admits he was not yet doing them: "To be honest, I'm not doing this yet, but I need to do it because I know it'll help a ton." (S012) By S001, S009 and S013 they are central, which suggests S012 is the earlier view (inferred from S001, S009, S012, S013).
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S003, S006, S009, S012, S013, S014).

## Pricing and raising prices
- **Stance:** Price for profit, not customer count. Raise until enough people say no; a very high close rate means you are too cheap. Never price off competitors once you have results.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "You want a price to make the most money not to get the most customers." (S004)
  - "If someone's willing to pay $20,000 and you only have one offer that's $8,000 and you're charging 8K, you didn't make 8K, you lost 12,000." (S002)
  - "the whole goal with pricing is to get someone to be uncomfortable with actually paying you." (S005)
  - "If you're charging only $3,000 for your service, then you're killing your business." (S013)
  - "If you get mediocre results, you can only charge mediocre prices." (S014)
  - "I raised my prices from $5,000 to $45,000." (S001)
- **Close-rate thresholds over time (all undated):**
  - Early course: "increase the price by $1,000 until you have a 30 to 35% closing rate with actual good closing skills." (S005)
  - Offer course: "every time you get three sales, you want to increase the price by 1/5 until you have a 35% closing rate with actual good closing skills." (S012)
  - Andres breakdown: "I would track the closing rate and if it's above a 50% I'd increase the price by 20% of the price." (S014) Sweet spot 35 to 40%.
  - Later video: "if you have anything above a 40% close rate, you need to be raising your prices" (S004), with "Every 90 days you should be raising the prices by 10 to 20%." (S004)
  - Elsewhere: "I don't want 80% closing rate." (S009) His own program reports "We have a 65% close rate on the people that we offer the program to." (S013), and his 10 metrics set close rate at 50% (S013).
- **Read:** the target band drifts from 30 to 35% up to 40 to 50% as a trigger. S005 is explicitly the early course and S004 looks latest (15K floor offer), so the newer rule is a time-based raise (every 90 days) gated by close rate plus profit per client and complaints (inferred from S004, S005, S012, S014). Note the tension: he raises above 40 to 50% but runs his own calls at 65% and treats 60% as bad (S001).
- **Competitors:** early: "just look at your competitors and charge what they're charging." (S005), for beginners without results. Later: "We don't want to be the average business. We want to be the best. We want to be the premium." (S004) S004 is newer.
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S009, S012, S013, S014, S011).

## Retention, ascension and LTV (backend)
- **Stance:** The money is in the second and third sale. Sell the next problem's solution, not a renewal, and fix LTV before you scale acquisition.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "instead of selling them a renewal offer, you should be selling them a new offer to help them solve a new problem because people don't want the same thing." (S002)
  - "99% of coaching businesses and consulting companies are broke. They sell once they turn and burn and within one or two years they're out of the industry" (S002)
  - "You can't let a client pay you just once and call it a day." (S004)
  - "the person that can keep the client to keep on buying will always win" (S004)
  - "your business should rely on monthly recurring revenue, MRR, not new clients." (S012)
  - "Before you even look into scaling, ask yourself, do you have a LTV you're happy with?" (S013)
- **Tension:** S012 builds on MRR renewal, while S002 says "consulting recurring revenue is going to be always low" (S002) and prefers an ascension ladder. S002 looks newer (his LTV ladder from 13K to $24,000, CSM on commission), so treat ascension as his current answer and renewals as a fallback priced at "minimum 4K 4K for the same duration" (S002) (inferred from S002, S012).
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S004, S006, S011, S012, S013, S014).

## Fulfilment and client delivery
- **Stance:** Delivery is a growth lever, not a cost centre. A first result earns the upsell and the referral, so the fulfilment team is a second sales team.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "I actually think that having better fulfillment is a higher revenue generating activity" (S001)
  - "I treat my fulfillment team as a second sales team straight up because my clients will only buy something else from me when we solve the first initial problem extremely well." (S002)
  - "do you see how everything revolves around the fulfillment?" (S009)
  - "Fulfillment over everything. Make sure your clients are happy and business will be so much easier." (S012)
  - "fulfillment is also part of a salesunnel." (S013)
  - "people make out fulfillment to be so much so much more complex than it needs to be" (S002)
- **Scalable or personal (views differ, undated):**
  - Personal: "Which is why my fulfillment will never be scalable and I'm completely fine with that." (S012) and "The last thing I want to do with my offer is productize it because that means that my client results will go down." (S012)
  - One-to-many: "most important thing is that you're doing one to many" (S002), with cohorts of 5 to 10, SOPs and a CSM holding 40 to 50 clients. In S014 the two lower price tiers are built to be scalable.
  - Read: S002 looks newer (bigger business, CSM team), so his current view is one-to-many with SOPs and paid CSMs, keeping one-on-ones for premium tiers (inferred from S002, S012, S014).
- **Burnout link:** higher price lowers delivery load: "when you charge more, naturally the better the ITP is going to be and naturally the less you're going to have to do in fulfillment" (S004) and "It's better to charge five people for 10K than to charge 50 people for like $1,000 because it's easier fulfillment." (S012)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S002, S004, S009, S012, S013, S014).

## Organic content versus paid ads
- **Stance (newer):** Both, but organic and brand first; ads last, and ads are built from organic winners.
- **Over time (undated):**
  - Early course: "You need to run paid ads to get your ideal clients looking at you." (S005)
  - Course era: "The easiest way to get to 50K per month is by running paid ads." (S007) and "you rely on the algorithm and sometimes you hit up they hit the jackpot, sometimes you don't and your business fluctuates" (S006)
  - Group calls: "Paid ads, the people that crush with organic will always crush with paid ads." (S013) and "take the exact hook, create a different body, and just test." (S013)
  - Later: "It's not ads versus organic. It's both." (S003) "That is the last lever you should pull right now." (S004) "People can copy your ads. People can copy your sales process. They can copy your fulfillment, but they can't copy your brand because that's built over years of work." (S001)
- **Read:** he moved from ads as the engine (S005, S007, where a media buyer runs the module) to brand and YouTube first with ads as an amplifier. S004 and S001 look newest (15K floor offer, 400K a month talk) (inferred from S001, S004, S005, S007, S013).
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S010, S013).

## Sales calls and closing
- **Stance:** The sale is made before the call. Get better at marketing, not closing, so the call has no objections and becomes an onboarding call.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "as an offer owner, the highest lever you can pull is to get better at marketing, not get better at closing" (S009)
  - "closing is a temporary solution to a long-term problem" (S009)
  - "your sales call should have zero objections" (S009)
  - "The sale is not done on the call. The sale is done before the call." (S014)
  - "when someone trusts you, they don't need a sales call, right?" (S001)
  - "Confidence in the way that you speak on a sales call is probably 50% of the sale already." (S012)
- **Earlier emphasis:** the early course put weight on closing skill: "If you don't, you're trash." (S005) about sub-30% closers. Later he says "I'm not a you know big sales guy." (S001) and buys sales training instead.
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S005, S007, S009, S011, S012, S013, S014).

## Post-booking and show rate
- **Stance:** The window between booking and the call is where the deal is won. Send long assets, phone 24 hours before, voice note the night before.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "the sale is made between the time they book a call and the time they actually take the call." (S007)
  - "make sure that he watches at least minimum two long firm assets, a VSSL and a client interview" (S014)
  - "booking in a call is the hardest commitment that a prospect has to make" (S008)
- **Threshold:** show rate standard 85% (S013); he called a fall from 92% to 63% an A+ problem worth about $31,200 (S001).
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S007, S008, S013, S014).

## DMs and appointment setting
- **Stance:** Founders should not set their own calls. Setters build rapport, send assets and push for a yes or no; more pain, not more pushing, gets the booking.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "it's 2024. If you're still setting your own appointments, you are behind." (S014)
  - "I just don't think there's enough pain in the conversation." (S009)
  - "I don't like maybe." (S004)
  - "We are always going to assume that they're busy cuz they are." (S004)
  - "So have less friction in the setting process and just get them on a call." (S011)
  - "Training your setter is one of the highest leverage activities that you can do as a business owner." (S010)
- **Threshold:** conversation to booked call: "Industry standard is around 5 to 10%. CM standard is around 15 to 20%." (S014) His 10 metrics set a floor of 5 in 100 (S013).
- **Push level:** "So the more BC your prospect is, the more unaware and the more less sophisticated they are, I'd push more because they need that extra kind of nudge." (S009)
- **Early version:** the early course revived past leads by DM: "These are the lowest hanging fruit, so these are the easiest sales to make." (S005)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S005, S009, S010, S011, S013, S014).

## Free value and free courses
- **Stance:** Give the best information away and sell implementation and access. Fear of giving too much means your fulfilment is not differentiated.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "If you're afraid of giving this too much then what that means is your fulfillment is not differentiated enough to where you help them with implementation." (S004)
  - "you want to give them the location, but you don't show them the GPS." (S004)
  - "Don't be afraid of providing enough free value. There's never enough free value" (S009)
  - "people perceived you based on the free value you give out." (S012)
  - "People don't buy information, they buy access to you and they buy implementation." (S013)
  - "your free content should be the blueprint." (S014)
- **Changed view:** he once charged for his best information and now calls that "But that is the worst piece of advice everyone like I've ever been told and I' I've ever listened to" (S013). Newer view is give it free.
- **Tension:** "show them what to do, but don't tell them how to do it" (S007) versus giving the blueprint away (S014). Both hold if the free piece teaches direction and early steps, and implementation stays paid (inferred from S004, S007, S014).
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S007, S009, S012, S013, S014).

- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "you have to pinpoint your A+ problem with data. you need to be tracking everything." (S001)

  - "this is by far the most important metric that you have to understand LTV to CAC." (S002)

  - "I'm concerned with my follower per view ratio." (S009)

This is consulting mastery standard." (S013)

## Offer design
- **Stance:** When ads or sales fail, check the offer first. Make it a category of one, solve every failure scenario with a deliverable, and give bonuses worth more than the core.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "If you don't get the offer right, no matter how good your marketing is, no matter how good your sales is, you'll never be able to scale up." (S005)
  - "it's not the ads that don't work. It's not the sales team that has the issue. It's usually your offer." (S005)
  - "if you're not making at least 30 50k per month, you don't get many referrals, and you have trouble scaling, then it's probably an offer issue, right?" (S012)
  - "I wouldn't touch anything on acquisition or like amplifying what's working right now until we fix the business economics." (S002)
  - "the value of bonuses should always be more than the core offer." (S005, S012)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S004, S005, S012, S013). Builds on Hormozi's value equation, credited in S012.

## High ticket versus low ticket
- **Stance:** No low ticket. A cheap tier dilutes the business and pulls the wrong buyer.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "it's way easier to scale a 4K offer than it is a $30 package." (S005)
  - "I don't have a low ticket. like our cheapest offer is 15K and it's intentionally built that way" (S004)
  - "I don't want you to make general public content for low ticket only. I never want you to do this because what this is doing is it's diluting your business." (S004)
  - "you have to sell exclusivity." (S014)
- **Exception:** a cheap done-for-you front end is fine as a funnel into the flagship: "Nobody wants to buy a ridiculously expensive consulting offer, but everyone wants to buy a cheap, done for you recruitment offer." (S002), but only "this is a play that you run once your systems are fully like fleshed out" (S002).
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S004, S005, S014).

## Discounting, downsells and payment plans
- **Stance:** Never discount; only raise prices. Collect cash up front, offer one option at a time.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "I'm only going to ever run a price increase." (S012)
  - "I'm never running one again because I attracted a lot of cheap people and that was my mistake." (S012), about one Black Friday discount.
  - "us humans optimize for hearing yes more than anything." (S004), why closers drift to cheap options.
  - "Cash upfront is always king." (S004)
  - "you only want to give them one option at a time and control the situation" (S004)
- **Inconsistent:** S014 has a 7K, 5K and 3K tier pitched in sequence after a no, "the $7,000 price point will act as a price anchor." (S014), and S002 runs a done-for-you downsell that rolls into the flagship. Yet in the same S014 breakdown he tells Andres to drop the cheapest tier: "I know for a fact that your closer is downelling people the moment that they get the the objection." (S014) The later S004 line (no low ticket, 15K floor) suggests the no-downsell view is newer (inferred from S002, S004, S012, S014).
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S004, S012, S014).

## Volume, consistency and patience
- **Stance:** Do volume until you see what works, then double down. Impatient with inputs, patient with results.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "You need to put out enough volume to actually see what works. And the moment that you see what works, you double down on it." (S010)
  - "if you do volume for two months, you will solve content." (S011)
  - "you got to be impatient with the inputs but patient with the results" (S009)
  - "if you're not willing to play the long-term game and you're not willing to do content for the long term, don't even try" (S009)
  - "It's not a matter of volume. It's not a matter that you didn't do it enough. You're just doing it wrong." (S006), the counterweight when targets are wrong.
- **Sources:** 5 (S006, S008, S009, S010, S011).

- **Evidence (undated):**

- **Limit:** "You're not ready to build a media team. You got to be able to spend to really want to build a media team." (S001) and "it's just going to be the blind leading the blind." (S001) before you know what works.
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S004, S010, S011, S013).

## Focus, bottlenecks and saying no
- **Stance:** One constraint at a time. Do the two or three highest revenue tasks, not twenty, and say no to the rest.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "The most important skill that you have to learn as a founder is not the marketing, it's not the sales, it's learning how to say no." (S001)
  - "If you could only do one thing to drive the most revenue to the business, what would that be?" (S001)
  - "Because constraints, there is always only one because businesses grow until the constraint hits and then it stops." (S011)
  - "there's no point of amplifying what's not currently working as efficient as it should be." (S002)
  - "No, stick to the basics." (S004)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S004, S011, S013).

## Work hours
- **Stance:** Fewer hours on higher leverage work. Compress calls into set windows.
- **Evidence (undated):**

  - "allows me to work four hours a day and scale faster than all of my competition" (S008)
  - "the truth is, I work four to five hours a day" (S011)

- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S004, S008, S011, S012, S009).

## Burnout
- **Stance:** Burnout comes from doing everything yourself and tying your dopamine to hours. The fix is systems, delegation and stillness, not more effort.
- **Evidence (undated):**
  - "The creators who burn out are doing everything themselves, and the brands that compound are the ones who build the systems to operate at scale." (S001)
  - "I was depressed out of my mind. I was working 14 hour days and I was only making 10K a month." (S011)
  - "You need to detach your dopamine from the amount of work you do every single day" (S011)
  - "I posted for 6 months and it got to a point where I felt like it it just wasn't working." (S003)
