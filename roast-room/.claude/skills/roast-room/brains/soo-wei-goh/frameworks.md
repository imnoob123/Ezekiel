# Frameworks: Soo Wei Goh

Built from the six batch notes on S001 to S014 (coaching calls, group calls, solo videos and course modules). 25 frameworks, most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes.

Dating note: all sources are undated. Rough order from internal clues (his stated age, offer prices, revenue): S005 is earliest (course at 18 or 19, 4K to 10K offers, paid ads first); S006 and S007 are early (4K to 8K offers, VSSL and ads funnel); S008 to S014 are middle (about 19 to 20, 100K to 224K months, 5K to 10K offers); S001 to S004 look latest (cheapest offer 15K, price raised to 45K, 400K a month, 408K at 21 (S003)). Inferred from S001, S003, S004, S005, S011, S012.

## 1. Ask buyers why they bought (buyer mirror)
- **What it is:** The foundation of his content and positioning is not the value he gives but why clients chose him. Ask paying clients, then build content and brand on the repeated answers.
- **Parts:** At onboarding, every client fills a form: where did you first find me, what content made you take me seriously, what was the last thing before you booked, main reason you reached out now, hesitations, why me over other coaches. Separately, book calls with your 3 to 10 favourite or pay-in-full clients, review your content together, and ask why until you reach the real reason (two to three whys, or five).
- **When to use:** Content is not converting, positioning feels generic, or you do not know which videos bring buyers.
- **How to score:** High if the founder can name the top three reasons clients chose them and the content that made them book, from client data. Low if content ideas come from the founder's head. He weights pay-in-full clients over payment-plan clients.
- **Quote:** "The foundation is not the value you provide. The foundation is what makes you different and why that client chose you." (S008) "You can solve 99% of your content problems by asking your client." (S010)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S003, S004, S006, S008, S010, S011, S013, S014)

## 2. Objections into content *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Every objection heard on a sales call or in DMs is a video that has not been made yet. The sales side decides what middle-of-funnel content gets filmed.
- **Parts:** Setters report the most-asked DM questions, closers log objections (he runs Fathom recordings through an AI prompt that ranks objections by frequency), a creative director turns them into topics. Match format to objection: client interview for "I need to think about it" or "will it work for my niche", own-story video for "I have been burned", expertise video for "can you help me".
- **When to use:** Calls have objections, close rate dips, or the content calendar is empty.
- **How to score:** High if each common objection has at least one published answer and calls are close to objection-free. Low if the same objection keeps appearing on calls. His target is a call with "zero objections" (S009).
- **Quote:** "Objections you hear on sales calls are absolute gold mines for content." (S009) "if you get a specific objection on a sales call nine times out of 10 it's because your content failed to do its job" (S003)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S006, S009, S010, S012, S013, S014)

## 3. Price raise by close rate *(unnamed; thresholds changed over time)*
- **What it is:** Price is set by testing the market, not by competitors. A high close rate means the price is too low.
- **Parts and thresholds by era:**
  - Early (S005): beginners charge what competitors charge; established coaches raise by $1,000 until close rate is 30 to 35% with good closing skills.
  - Middle (S012): every three sales, raise by one fifth until close rate is about 35%. His story: $7,000 at 100% close, then 8.5K at 45 to 50%, then down to 35%.
  - Middle (S014): above 50% close, raise 20%; sweet spot 35 to 40%; aim to hear more nos than yeses.
  - Later (S004): every 90 days raise 10 to 20%; above 40% close, raise; keep the new price if profit per client rises and complaints stay low; if sales stop entirely, step back.
  - Conflicting metric: S013 lists a 50% close rate as a program KPI, and S013 reports 65% on those offered. S001 says a 60% closer is "really bad", S009 says he does not want 80%.
- **When to use:** Any pricing decision, and any time close rate is high.
- **How to score:** Guidance: close rate above 50% means underpriced; 35 to 40% is the target band; under 30% on a modest price means a closing or offer problem, not a price problem. The newer view (S004) adds a slower cadence and a profit-per-client check, so prefer it for an established business.
- **Quote:** "every time you get three sales, you want to increase the price by 1/5 until you have a 35% closing rate with actual good closing skills." (S012) "if you have anything above a 40% close rate, you need to be raising your prices" (S004)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S004, S005, S009, S012, S013, S014)

## 4. Free value: the location but not the GPS (value debt)
- **What it is:** Give the best information away free, show what to do but not how to implement it, and sell implementation and access. Free value creates "house money" and a sense of owing.
- **Parts:** List the client's problems in order. Free content (often a long free course) fully solves the first few and only points the direction for the rest. Implementation, accountability and proximity sit behind the paywall. A setter then DMs people who consumed the free material.
- **When to use:** Deciding what to give away, launching a lead magnet, or killing a cheap DIY product.
- **How to score:** High if the free asset solves real early problems and the paid offer is clearly implementation and access. Low if the founder hoards information or sells a low-ticket DIY version of the free material. His own test: fear of giving too much away means fulfilment is not differentiated.
- **Quote:** "you want to give them the location, but you don't show them the GPS." (S004) "People don't buy information, they buy access to you and they buy implementation." (S013)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S007, S009, S011, S012, S013, S014)

## 5. Top, middle and bottom of funnel content *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Content split by job. Top earns attention and likability, middle builds trust, expertise and problem awareness, bottom confirms the decision with case studies and client interviews.
- **Parts:** Start from a limiting-belief map: list every belief stopping a prospect buying, write the opposite belief, then build a video format for each. Read the lead tracker: too few cold leads means top of funnel, too few warm means middle, too few hot means bottom. If ads already bring attention, film bottom of funnel first. He cites the five stages of awareness for the middle.
- **When to use:** Planning a content calendar, or diagnosing which stage is leaking.
- **How to score:** Guidance: score each stage against its lead count. High if the content mix follows the weakest stage; low if a founder posts only top-of-funnel because they do not track sales data.
- **Quote:** "Organic content as a whole is actually very simple. You essentially just want to map out every single belief that's holding your client, your prospect back from working with you" (S001) "your lack of tracking on your sales part is the reason why you're always posting top of funnel content." (S004)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S004, S009, S011, S013)

## 6. Media team and brand director *(unnamed; he names the roles)*
- **What it is:** A founder only supplies ideas and on-camera time; a team owned by a head of content or brand director does the rest, run like a sales team with its own numbers.
- **Parts:** Smallest version: creative director, YouTube editor, short-form editor (S010) or first three hires of editor, setter at 5% commission, VA at about $10 an hour (S011). Full version: head of content, long-form editors, thumbnail designer, short-form editors, full-time clipper, all under 15K a month (S001). Base plus per-piece pay; editor bonuses of $50 per sale from a qualified content lead, $50 per 100 qualified followers, $500 for the monthly reach goal (S009). Weekly content meeting with winners feeding new ideas.
- **When to use:** Founder is editing, scripting or uploading; output is below one reel a day.
- **How to score:** High if the founder only approves ideas and films, and each content hire owns a number. Low if the founder edits video. He says do not build the team before you know what works, and only if you will spend.
- **Quote:** "you can literally hire every single one of these people in these positions for less than 15K a month comfortably." (S001) "if you get one editor to do both YouTube and short form, you'll never be good at both." (S010)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S004, S009, S010, S011)

## 7. Ascension ladder and back-end LTV
- **What it is:** Every solution creates a new problem. Sell the next solution instead of a renewal, so lifetime value rises without new traffic.
- **Parts:** On the onboarding call map the client's year (month 1, 3, 6, 9, 12 problems). Build a premium at about 2x the flagship (one-on-ones, in-person events, done-for-you), or an upsell at 2 to 3x core. Continuations, if any, at least 4K for the same term. A CSM books every meeting in advance and is paid commission on upsells. In-person events sit inside the premium offer.
- **When to use:** Revenue swings month to month, clients leave after one program, or front-end cash carries the business.
- **How to score:** Guidance from his numbers: LTV to CAC around 8:1 is workable and 12:1 is the goal (S002); in S013 his target LTV is about 6K with 15% upsell and 30% cross-sell. Low score if there is one offer and no backend.
- **Quote:** "when you give someone a solution to a problem, there's always going to be a new problem that comes from the new solution." (S004) "instead of selling them a renewal offer, you should be selling them a new offer to help them solve a new problem because people don't want the same thing." (S002)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S004, S011, S012, S013)

## 8. Dream follower and the top-ICP sweep
- **What it is:** Two audience rules that work at different stages of the funnel. Top of funnel speaks to the "dream follower", a much larger group who share the ICP's hobbies and ambitions, so the algorithm pushes it wide and more ICP arrive. Middle and bottom speak to the most sophisticated, highest-LTV buyer, so smaller buyers believe they can follow.
- **Parts:** Name the ICP (he writes to one persona, "John"), name the dream follower around them, film top-of-funnel hooks that appeal to both, then narrow with niche words after the hook. Keep a main channel for broad value and a second channel for ICP-only topics. Never make general content only for low-ticket buyers.
- **When to use:** Views are high but revenue is flat (wrong audience), or reach is too small (ICP too narrow).
- **How to score:** Rising views with flat revenue means the content talks to the wrong people. High if top-of-funnel grows qualified followers and middle-of-funnel brings the top 20% buyer.
- **Quote:** "You shouldn't make content for your ICP. You should make content for your actual dream follower." (S008) "The only person I want you to speak to is the highest sophistication level because that will essentially sweep everybody down the room." (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S003, S004, S008, S010)

## 9. Hooks and packaging first
- **What it is:** The hook, title and thumbnail decide a video, so they get most of the effort and are made before filming.
- **Parts:** Three hooks (title, visual, verbal). Hook must be controversial, feel like talking to them, or promise a desired result, inside 3 to 5 seconds. Title and hook must match. Interest peaks (one-line teasers) through the body. Package before filming; never outsource title, thumbnail and hook idea to an editor.
- **When to use:** Reviewing a script, reel or YouTube plan.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 80 to 90% of scripting time on the hook. For YouTube he tracks only click-through rate (5 to 10%) and average view duration (about 15% viewed) (S006).
- **Quote:** "You should focus 80 to 90% of your time on the hook because if you have a trash hook, you have a trash video." (S007) "I want you to essentially make sure that you have three hooks. A title hook, visual hook, and a verbal hook." (S003)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S003, S006, S007, S010)

## 10. Steal like an artist (twist on a proven format)
- **What it is:** Find outlier formats and hooks that already worked, then rebuild them with your own twist. His view changed on how close to copy.
- **Parts:** List 10 to 30 creators in the niche, pull their outliers, keep the structure, combine parts from several creators, add the twist that matches why your clients chose you, pick topics hard to replicate. Reuse your own winning hook on a new ending.
- **Changed view:** In S006 he tells the story of copying a viral hook nearly word for word and says early copying is fine to find a voice. In S007 he says never copy word for word and admits "I was guilty of this myself." S003, S010 and S011 keep the twist line and add that copying a bigger creator caps you. S007 looks newer than the S006 story because it names the old habit as a mistake.
- **When to use:** Low views, or a founder asking what to post.
- **How to score:** High if a format is proven and visibly adapted; low if copied verbatim, or if someone is clearly better at that format (then skip it, S010).
- **Quote:** "you want to steal certain things from different creators and combine them together so you steal like an artist." (S007) "I took a format that worked. I understood how I could actually have that twist on it and I made it different." (S003)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S006, S007, S009, S010)

## 11. Touch points across platforms (7-11-4 rule)
- **What it is:** Buyers need hours of content and many touches before booking. Instagram earns attention, YouTube builds trust, DMs and stories convert.
- **Parts:** About 7 hours with the brand, 11 touch points, 4 platforms (S009); a later version says over 7 hours, 14 touch points, 3 platforms (S014). His touches: reels, stories, setter DMs, 20 to 30 minute YouTube videos. Setters send 20 to 30 YouTube links a day.
- **When to use:** High-trust, high-skepticism buyers; deciding YouTube versus Instagram.
- **How to score:** Guidance: mastering two of four touch points doubles the business (S009). He weights YouTube heavily: 6,000 subscribers drove 80% of his sales (S010).
- **Quote:** "To make a purchase, a consumer needs to engage with a brand for 7 hours, have 11 touch points, and encounter encounter the brand across four different platforms." (S009) "Everything I do on is IG is to get someone on YouTube and then once they're on YouTube, they DM me back on IG." (S011)
- **Sources:** 5 (S006, S009, S010, S011, S014)

## 12. Metrics stack and reverse-engineered targets
- **What it is:** Work back from the cash target to daily activity, track a fixed set of numbers, and give every team member one number.
- **Parts:** The 10 crucial metrics (S013): qualified ICP in per day 10 to 30; ICP reached out to about 30 a day; conversation to booking at least 5 in 100; show rate 85%; offer rate 7 of 10; close rate 50%; cash collected to revenue 60 to 70%; LTV about 6K; upsell 15%; cross-sell 30%. Setting KPI: new conversations to booked calls, industry 5 to 10%, his program 15 to 20% (S014). ICP-lead math: about 2 to 3 sales per 10 ICP leads (S004). Reverse engineering: 100K target at 5K AOV is 20 closes (S010). He says his thresholds are his program's standard, not industry.
- **When to use:** Any growth plan, hire, or "what is wrong" question.
- **How to score:** No tracking scores low by default; he refuses to advise without data. Score each metric against these bands as guidance.
- **Quote:** "Not your new followers, your qualified ICP." (S013) "If your average order value is say 5K, that means you need 20 closes to generate 100K." (S010)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S004, S010, S013, S014)

## 13. Client interviews as the main sales asset
- **What it is:** Client interviews, case study breakdowns and client journey videos convert better than any other content, and should be packaged to look like value content.
- **Parts:** Open with the transformation number, then problems, story, and the salesy questions last. One interview per ICP and niche, because you attract people like the interviewee. Film many at masterminds. Clips or live reactions, never screenshots.
- **When to use:** "I need to think about it" objections, entering a new niche, low trust.
- **How to score:** High if there is at least one interview per ICP segment. He credits about half his clients joining to interviews (S009). Note he admits in S012 he was not doing them yet.
- **Quote:** "client interviews are the best ways to sign clients. This is by far the most important sales asset." (S001) "the I need to think about it objection is purely a lack of proof. It's purely a lack of proof." (S009)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S006, S009, S012, S014)

## 14. Highest revenue generating activity and the single constraint
- **What it is:** A business has one constraint at a time. Find it with data, put the first hours of the day on it, and stop adding work beside it.
- **Parts:** Ask what one activity drives most revenue and why it is not half your day. Compute a dollar-per-hour rate (his: $2,243) and judge tasks against it. A+ problem protocol: price the problem (show rate 92% to 63% cost about $31,200), set a 3-day plan with an owner per action and daily stand-ups. His priority order: content, then fulfilment, then sales; ads last. Test: would the 100K-a-month version of me do this task?
- **When to use:** Founder is busy but revenue is flat; too many priorities.
- **How to score:** High if the founder names one constraint and spends most time on it; low if they list ten problems or work a 20-task to-do list.
- **Quote:** "If you could only do one thing to drive the most revenue to the business, what would that be?" (S001) "Because constraints, there is always only one because businesses grow until the constraint hits and then it stops." (S011)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S004, S011, S013)

## 15. Offer build *(irresistible offer)*
- **What it is:** If ads or sales are not converting, the offer is usually the problem. Build it from market research so it is a category of one.
- **Parts:** Four variables: extreme pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing market. Dream outcome sentence: I help [outcome] in [time] without [biggest pain]. Market research calls with 10 to 15 followers. Client failure scenario sheet: list every way the client could fail and a solution for each, then about five deliverables that cover them. 10x and one-tenth price questions. Niche ladder (same product at $20, $500, $2,000 as the audience narrows). Unique mechanism so nobody can compare you.
- **When to use:** Below 30 to 50K a month, few referrals, trouble scaling; or a new offer for the next problem.
- **How to score:** Guidance: high if every failure scenario has a deliverable and the offer cannot be compared with a competitor's. Low if built without research.
- **Quote:** "If you don't get the offer right, no matter how good your marketing is, no matter how good your sales is, you'll never be able to scale up." (S005) "I listed out every single client failure scenario." (S012)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S005, S012, S013)

## 16. VSSL and the post-booking process
- **What it is:** The sale is made between booking and the call. A video sales letter and a post-booking sequence pre-sell, so calls become onboarding calls.
- **Parts:** VSSL (S007): call-out hook with three "without" pains, origin story, failure, epiphany, transformation, opportunity, client results, who it is for, why past attempts failed, why yours is one of one, named process, next step. About 70% about you; do not sell the process in it. Post-booking (S014, five steps): continue the chat with a personal question, send the VSSL then a client interview, setter phones 24 hours before, voice note the night before, 15 minutes of research before the call.
- **When to use:** Low show rate, long calls, many objections on calls.
- **How to score:** Guidance: target show rate about 85 to 90% and at least two long assets watched before the call.
- **Quote:** "The sale is not done on the call. The sale is done before the call." (S014) "the sale is made between the time they book a call and the time they actually take the call." (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S006, S007, S010, S014)

## 17. Brand trust stages *(several of his models)*
- **What it is:** People buy coaches, not coaching, so content must make them admire, like and trust the person before shifting beliefs.
- **Parts:** Four stages of a brand: admirable, likable, credible, trustable (S001); three pillars (S003). Expert versus friend Venn: teach and connect at once (S006). Teach, show, share, give content types (S006). Three trust ratings scored 1 to 10: trust as a person, trust in the mechanism, belief the solution fits (S006). Instagram's three jobs: like me, want my lifestyle, believe I can help (S009).
- **When to use:** Leads come but do not buy, or a founder only posts value.
- **How to score:** Ask the founder to rate the three trust questions 1 to 10; any low score names the content to film. A pure "value brand" scores low.
- **Quote:** "The first stage is being admirable. Second stage is being likable. Third stage is being credible. And the fourth stage is being trustable." (S001) "How can you be an expert but also be a friend? How can you teach but also connect with them?" (S006)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S003, S006, S009)

## 18. Pitch structure: one price at a time *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Control the call. Pitch one price, anchor high, and move down only after a no; never show all options.
- **Parts:** Close call: intro, current state, desire state, gap, pain, presentation, price drop, about 20 minutes (S013). Three tiers of one offer (7K, 5K, 3K) pitched top first as an anchor (S014). Price objection: confirm belief it works, isolate the objection, tie to the personal why, offer split payments. Payment plan total above pay-in-full.
- **When to use:** Writing a sales script or setting tiers.
- **How to score:** Low if the founder shows a menu of prices or the closer downsells by reflex.
- **Quote:** "you only want to give them one option at a time and control the situation" (S004) "Intro, current state, desire state, gap, pain, presentation, price drop." (S013)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S005, S013, S014)

## 19. Bonuses and guarantees
- **What it is:** Bonuses should be worth more than the core offer; guarantees show confidence but his preferred type changed.
- **Parts:** Bonuses: tools and checklists over extra training, a price tag on each, solve problems they will hit later. Guarantees: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee (only for big names, about 100K a month and up).
- **Changed view:** Early (S005) he backs bold unconditional guarantees as a calculated risk and says no guarantee is a lack of confidence. Later (S012) he does not recommend unconditional ("I had to learn the hard way") and uses a conditional one. In S009 he says publicly he does not promise a guarantee. S012 and S009 look newer.
- **When to use:** Building or fixing an offer.
- **How to score:** Guidance: conditional guarantee tied to attendance and work, bonuses valued above core.
- **Quote:** "the value of bonuses should always be more than the core offer." (S012) "I don't recommend unconditional cuz that's that's just going to attract a bunch of headache." (S012)
- **Sources:** 3 (S005, S009, S012)

## 20. Offer sheet
- **What it is:** A short document shown on screen at the end of a sales call that ties deliverables, structure, bonuses and guarantee together, so the price drop feels logical. Also used to design a new offer for the next problem.
- **Parts:** Deliverables, coaching structure, process, bonuses, guarantees (S005). For a new offer: outcome, point A to B, pains, blockers, a deliverable for each blocker, bonuses as nice-to-haves (S002).
- **When to use:** Building a call close or a new offer.
- **How to score:** High if every blocker has a deliverable and the sheet is screen-shared before price.
- **Quote:** "you just want to create a two to three page document on what exactly your program includes." (S005) "That's how you create an offer sheet." (S002)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S005, S012)

## 21. Delivery stack and client success cadence
- **What it is:** Fulfilment designed so clients get results, stay, renew and buy again.
- **Parts:** For a 90 to 120 day program (S012): warm-up roadmap call, a cash-injection first week, two weekly group calls, one-on-one "unstuck" calls capped at an hour, Loom replies within 12 hours, CSM weekly check-ins for 60 days, public wins, leaderboard, sub-communities, then renewal. CSM pod (S002): meetings booked from meetings, one CSM to 40 to 50 clients, base plus upsell commission. Cohorts of 5 to 10 run one-to-many. Client scoring (S013): track attendance and pick the next case study. Exit interviews for non-renewals.
- **When to use:** Churn, burnout from one-on-ones, or weak case studies.
- **How to score:** Guidance: high if there is a cadence, an owner, and a scoring system; low if delivery depends on the founder's memory.
- **Quote:** "you need to provide minimum two weekly group calls, tons of one-on-one support, and an amazing warm-up call." (S012) "Book a meeting from a meeting." (S002)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S012, S013)

## 22. Story strategy *(10K story strategy, four story types)*
- **What it is:** Instagram stories, not reels, drive much of his DM revenue. Stories document and show; reels give value.
- **Parts:** Early weekly plan (S005): Monday hard CTA with client result, Tuesday belief-breaking value, Wednesday personal story, Thursday own win, Friday soft CTA freebie. Later (S012, S013): four types (personality, documentation, social proof, CTA), two to three a day, speak to one named person, kill every sales objection, be yourself. Seven days of stories in 30 minutes.
- **When to use:** Warm audience but few DMs.
- **How to score:** High if stories run daily across the four types; low if the founder only posts reels and hopes.
- **Quote:** "I call it the 10K story strategy because usually when I launch a CTA, I make 10K per CTA." (S005) "Stories is for documenting and showing, not giving." (S013)
- **Sources:** 3 (S005, S012, S013)

## 23. A players: hiring, alignment and retention
- **What it is:** Hire on character, align the hire's goals with the business number, hold the standard, and keep a pipeline so firing is safe.
- **Parts:** Stallions, camels, donkeys: hire a camel and train them up (S001). Five traits: hardworking, no ego, growth over money, young, idolises you (S013). Defensiveness test: tell the candidate good work was bad and watch. Setter pipeline: 50 to 100 applicants, interviews, mock role play, hire two at once (S010). Alignment onboarding: their 6-month income goal turned into KPIs. Retain with weekly personal calls and trips.
- **When to use:** Any hire, especially setters and content leads.
- **How to score:** Low if hiring on skill alone, one setter with no backup, or KPIs framed as the owner's goal.
- **Quote:** "What I've personally found to be the best is to hire a camel and train them up to be a stallion." (S001) "my personal favorite expectation to set is that you are patient with people but ruthless with standards" (S010)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S010, S013)

## 24. Value equation (from Hormozi)
- **What it is:** Value equals dream outcome times perceived likelihood of success, divided by time delay times effort and sacrifice. He credits Hormozi in S012.
- **Parts:** His twist: first push time delay and effort toward zero (done-for-you without operational drag, templates, one-on-ones), then raise the top with client results.
- **When to use:** Raising price without lowering close rate; deciding what done-for-you to add.
- **How to score:** Guidance: rate each of the four parts; client results are the main lever on perceived likelihood.
- **Quote:** "Dream outcome times perceived likelihood of success divided by the time delay times the effort of sacrifice equals value." (S005)
- **Sources:** 2 (S005, S012)

## 25. Revive past leads *(cash injection)*
- **What it is:** Unsold calls from the last 12 months are the easiest sales.
- **Parts:** Reply to a story or send a voice note, no incentive, ask if they hit their goal, act surprised if not, ask what is stopping them, propose a call.
- **When to use:** Need fast cash; first week of a client program.
- **How to score:** High if the founder has a list of past no-buys and works it before buying ads.
- **Quote:** "These are the lowest hanging fruit, so these are the easiest sales to make." (S005)
- **Sources:** 1 (S005)
