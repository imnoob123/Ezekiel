# Stories: Soo Wei Goh

Built from the six extract batches over S001 to S014. 20 stories and cases he reuses, most-used first, with the lesson he draws. All sources are undated. Revenue figures are his own claims and are not verified.

1. **The free course that keeps paying.** He released free courses and each was followed by a record month; one made over 300K by his estimate and competitors answered with weaker ones, and run as an ad it returned 14x in 30 days: "You can't fake a free course like that." (S014) Lesson: give the best material away and sell implementation (S004, S009, S014).
2. **The ads-versus-organic objection.** A record month slipped when only two of the last ten calls closed, all saying they would focus on ads first. He made 3 reels and 2 YouTube videos on it and stopped hearing it. Lesson: an objection on calls is content you have not made yet (S001, S003).
3. **The hamster wheel.** He posted daily and scripted everything for six months until it felt like nothing worked, and in 2023 he says "I was working 14 hour days and I was only making 10K a month." (S011) Lesson: burnout comes from doing everything yourself; build a team and systems (S003, S011).
4. **The pedestalled 100K.** His first 100K month at 19 felt empty, and he later sat at 50K a month for six months while chasing 100K. Once he stopped pedestalising it he hit 100K and then 150K in 30 days. Lesson: stillness and the right inputs beat effort (S004, S011).
5. **Telling his mum.** A reel of him telling his mother he hit his first 100K, filmed as part of a vlog and not for reach, became a proof moment clients copy; he later posted a 60-day streak of daily calls to his parents. Lesson: capture real moments your buyers also want (S003, S008, S012).
6. **Emma and the back end.** Emma grew from 30K to 100K a month, then to 240K in 7 months after adding a back-end upsell with the same content at double the volume. Lesson: ascension, not new traffic (S003, S004).
7. **Paying to learn.** He paid over $200,000 to learn content systems from top brands and SOPed them, paid 20K for access to Daniel Dalen and went from 25K to 64K followers in three months, and bought Hormozi offers at 6K and 18K without a sales call. Lesson: pay the best, reverse-engineer, and notice that a brand which does not feel like selling closes before the call (S001, S010).
8. **Copying a viral hook.** He copied a rising creator's hook nearly word for word and went viral on his second post, then later admitted "I was guilty of this myself." (S007) about word-for-word stealing. Lesson: copy structure, add a twist, never the words (S006, S007).
9. **The 7K that closed every call.** He closed 100% of his first five calls at $7,000, read that as too cheap, raised to 8.5K and then until close rate fell to about 35% so he could deliver well for fewer clients. Elsewhere: "I raised my prices from $5,000 to $45,000." (S001) Lesson: a high close rate is a pricing signal (S001, S012).
10. **The show rate crash.** Show rate fell from 92% to 63% for two weeks. He did the math (about $31,200 lost), set a 3-day plan with an owner per action and daily stand-ups, and got close rate back to about 85%. Lesson: do the math before you decide a problem is too small (S001).
11. **John's 40 to 60K in two weeks.** A client bought a $25,000 flagship, then a $15,000 creative director offer, a 10K VIP ticket and 10K more of one-on-ones, because more offers existed. His own LTV went from 13K to $24,000 in 3 months, and his CSM earned $19K in a month on upsell commission. Lesson: the sale starts once they say yes (S002).
12. **Alessio and Brian drop the cheap tier.** On 4K, 8K and 12K offers with a 60% close rate, he had them remove the lowest option and raise price; they went from a 37K record month to on track for 250K in 10 months. Lesson: premium pricing plus better marketing (S004).
13. **Andres pauses acquisition.** A DPDR coach at 600K in 12 months had no tracking, a DIY blueprint product and a closer who downsold. Andres paused acquisition to fix fulfilment, Soo Wei told him "Get rid of the blueprint program." (S014), and he hit his second 100K month. Lesson: fix delivery and tracking before more leads (S014).
14. **One interview per ICP.** After his client interview with a TikTok shop coach, five TikTok shop coaches joined that month; one client-interview-as-value video drove over $120,000 directly. Lesson: film an interview for every buyer type you want (S001, S009).
15. **Where the big payers came from.** About 80% of the buyers who paid 12 to 18K up front came from YouTube, and "my 6,000 subscribers contribute to 80% of my sales." (S010) Lesson: YouTube carries high-ticket trust (S009, S010).
16. **The editor and the competitor.** An agency gave a competitor the same edit as his, so he paid his editors double to come in-house. Lesson: your edge must not be copyable (S001).
17. **A month off.** He took a month off and the business did 370K, up from 320K, on a content team costing 15K a month. Lesson: A players and systems let the founder step back (S001).
18. **The setter who left.** His first setter churned and it took three months to replace; revenue fell from 50K to 20K. Lesson: keep a talent pipeline so high standards are affordable (S013).
19. **The Black Friday regret.** He ran one discount and says "I attracted a lot of cheap people and that was my mistake." (S012) He also turned down coaches ready to pay 5 to 7K when his product was weak. Lesson: never discount, and protect reputation over cash (S012).
20. **Jeremy Park's research call.** Market research showed a 4M-follower fitness creator's audience wanted to become influencers, not get shredded; skipping research would have sunk the launch. Lesson: research before you build an offer (S012).
